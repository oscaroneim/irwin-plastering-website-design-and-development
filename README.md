# Irwin Plastering Website Design & Development

**Independent Client Project | Completed April 2025**

**Role:** Sole Designer & Developer  
**Stack:** Next.js 15 · React 19 · TypeScript · Tailwind CSS · Zod · React Hook Form · EmailJS · Vercel  
**Design:** Figma · Responsive UI · Wireframing · Brand Development  
**Status:** Completed - Original domain currently inactive

## Project Overview

Designed, developed and deployed a responsive website for an independent plastering business, taking ownership of the project from initial discovery through to technical handover.

Working from a single physical advertisement and a limited Instagram presence, I conducted competitor research, planned the website's information architecture, explored wireframes and developed responsive designs in Figma. I translated an initially ambiguous brief into a structured user experience and a functional website.

I independently handled frontend development, form validation, customer enquiry integration, technical SEO, deployment and documentation.

The project was designed around the client's limited budget, prioritising maintainability, performance and avoiding unnecessary backend infrastructure.

### What was achieved

- **End-to-end ownership:** Managed discovery, design, development, deployment and handover.
- **UI/UX design:** Conducted competitor benchmarking, planned information architecture and created responsive Figma designs.
- **Frontend engineering:** Built the website using Next.js, React and TypeScript.
- **Form integration:** Implemented Zod validation, React Hook Form and EmailJS.
- **Technical SEO:** Configured sitemap generation, robots.txt and SEO fundamentals.
- **Infrastructure:** Configured Vercel deployment and domain DNS.
- **Stakeholder management:** Translated unclear requirements into approved designs through iterative feedback.

## The Challenge

The project began without a detailed technical brief, established website or comprehensive brand guidelines.

I needed to establish the website's structure, visual direction and content while working with a non-technical stakeholder. The design approval process extended over several months, requiring multiple iterations and clear communication of design decisions.

The client also had a limited budget, making ongoing running costs and maintainability important considerations.

## Discovery & Design

As the sole designer and developer, I was responsible for shaping the website's structure, user experience and visual direction before implementing it. I used stakeholder questions, competitor benchmarking, Figma and FigJam to work from a limited brief towards a responsive interface focused on helping prospective customers understand the services and make an enquiry.

### 1. Stakeholder Discovery & Requirements

I began by preparing questions to clarify the business's priorities rather than assuming what the website should contain. These covered the intended audience (commercial clients, private houses and homeowners), services offered, operating area, preferred contact methods, existing marketing channels, available project photographs and the business's distinguishing qualities.

The discussions established practical constraints and content needs. For example, understanding the focus on Buckinghamshire and Oxfordshire and the different customer groups informed the regional messaging and service presentation I explored in later designs.

### 2. Competitor Benchmarking & UI Inspiration

I researched plastering and construction websites to understand how similar businesses structured their navigation, described services, presented completed projects and encouraged visitors to get in touch. In FigJam, I grouped references by interface area including navigation bars, service pages, project galleries, company information and contact forms so I could compare specific patterns rather than just collect whole-page screenshots.

I also explored contact-form and layout inspiration on Dribbble, looking at approaches to field grouping, service selection, typography and visual hierarchy. For visual direction, I reviewed construction-sector branding and found Jewson a useful reference for the client's industry. These were **third-party references**, not designs I created or copied directly.

This research helped me identify options to explore in my own layouts; it was competitor benchmarking and visual research, **not formal usability testing or user interviews**.

### 3. Information Architecture & Enquiry Journey

Using the requirements and research, I drafted an initial sitemap covering the homepage, services, projects, testimonials, company information and contact page. I also sketched navigation and footer arrangements, including a prominent quote call to action and alternative routes to contact details.

The sitemap was a **planning exploration**, rather than a claim that every proposed page or feature was included in the delivered site. Its purpose was to establish a coherent content hierarchy and make it easier for different customer groups to find relevant services and reach the enquiry form.

## Engineering & Architecture

### Selected Code Examples & Refactoring

The original website was delivered in April 2025. Since completing the project, I have revisited parts of the implementation to apply what I've learned about maintainability, type safety and separation of concerns.

The examples in this repository represent **post-project refactoring work**, rather than the exact code deployed in the original website.

**Areas of focus:**

- **Validation:** Extracting the Zod schema, form types and reusable option constants into a dedicated module.
- **Form architecture:** Separating React Hook Form state management from validation rules and presentation.
- **Maintainability:** Reducing duplicated values and making individual responsibilities easier to understand and test.

These examples are intended to demonstrate my engineering approach and continued development. They do not constitute a complete, independently runnable copy of the original application.

### Next.js and React
I selected Next.js for its support for static generation, SEO metadata and image optimisation. Its React-based architecture also supported reusable UI components.

### TypeScript
I used TypeScript throughout the project to improve type safety, catch errors earlier and make the codebase more maintainable. The project also provided an opportunity to strengthen my practical TypeScript experience.

### Forms: Zod, React Hook Form and EmailJS
I used Zod for schema-based input validation and React Hook Form for form state and error handling.

EmailJS handled customer enquiries and automated confirmation emails without requiring a dedicated backend email service.

### Cost-Conscious Architecture
The website did not require a database or custom server-side application logic, so I took a static-first approach to avoid unnecessary infrastructure.

I used Vercel for hosting and GitHub-connected deployment, with the domain managed through GoDaddy. The goal was to minimise maintenance and recurring costs while keeping the solution straightforward for the client.

### SEO
I planned an SEO-friendly site structure and configured technical SEO fundamentals, including sitemap and robots.txt generation using next-sitemap.

## Deployment & Handover

## What I Learned

This project strengthened my ability to manage the full delivery lifecycle, from understanding business requirements and exploring design solutions through to frontend implementation, deployment and handover.

One of the most valuable lessons was the importance of planning the user experience before development. Researching comparable websites, exploring content structures and creating wireframes helped me think beyond individual components and consider how the overall experience would work for prospective customers.

I also gained experience translating ambiguous requirements into design decisions, presenting ideas to a non-technical stakeholder and refining the interface through feedback.

From an engineering perspective, the project reinforced the importance of selecting technology appropriate to the problem rather than introducing unnecessary complexity.

Revisiting parts of the implementation after delivery has also given me an opportunity to reflect on how I can improve component structure, type safety and maintainability as my frontend development skills continue to develop.
